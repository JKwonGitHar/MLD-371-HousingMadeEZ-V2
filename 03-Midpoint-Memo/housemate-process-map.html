How Harvard affiliates find a housemate in Boston today

This map follows one person, an incoming international student, fellow or researcher, from deciding to share a place to living with someone and, when a housemate leaves, starting the search again. The top lane is what the person experiences. The lanes below show the work underneath: where they look, how they sort and check people, what the landlord and lease require, and what happens once they live together. Each badge marks a kind of friction: some is a cost to cut, some is the reason the household works and should stay. Dashed boxes are side channels that skip every check.

Two kinds of friction in the housemate search

Friction that is a cost

  • Typing the same "looking for a housemate" intro into every group chat, then scrolling hundreds of messages for a match.
  • Waiting days for a reply across time zones, before you have even arrived.
  • Gathering the same credit, income and guarantor documents again for every landlord.
  • Paying a stranger who "holds the lease" before you have met them or the landlord.

Friction that is high value

  • The long talk about money, cleanliness, guests and sleep. Nobody else can have it for you.
  • A friend or classmate vouching for someone: a smaller pool, but a trusted one.
  • Everyone signing the lease, so each "yes" is a real commitment with tenant rights behind it.
  • Writing down house rules together. Skip it and the household moves in faster but breaks down sooner.
