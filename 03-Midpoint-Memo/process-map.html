How international students furnish an unfurnished apartment today

This map traces the full process, top to bottom, from agreeing to rent an unfurnished unit to living with the furniture and eventually moving out. Furniture usually becomes a problem only after the lease is signed and the student has moved in, so most of the search happens while they are already living in an empty room. Whether a student has a roommate isn't guaranteed, so the map splits there: with a roommate, they agree on shared items and split the cost; without one, they choose and pay for everything alone. Students then split six ways to find furniture, and three ways to move it, before inspecting, paying, getting tools, assembling and disposing of what's left. Two loops matter: items that don't fit or arrive damaged send students back to the search, and furniture left behind at move-out becomes next year's second-hand and curbside supply, so the process is really a cycle. Red steps are friction that costs students time, money or safety, and gold steps are friction worth keeping because it prevents a bigger problem. The team first marked three (items not as advertised, hidden shipping fees, and mandatory dimension checks); the rest were identified on review. The costs cluster around money and timing: upfront lease costs, the September 1 rush, and payment apps that need a U.S. bank account. Delays on the arrows are typical estimates, not measured times.
