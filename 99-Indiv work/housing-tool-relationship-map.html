International student housing tool: relationship map

How data sources, analysis, the tool, and the housing ecosystem connect — with sources backing each entity and issue.

1. Data sources feeding the tool

Four Harvard-affiliated student groups feed survey data into an AI-assisted analysis process, which informs development of the housing tool. No single group or board owns this data — sources are informal and ongoing.

2. Tool, students, and the housing ecosystem

The tool serves international students, who interact with Harvard University Housing and off-campus landlords across Cambridge and Boston, and helps address seven categories of housing-related issues.

Scams targeting international students

International students are frequent rental-scam targets because they're often unfamiliar with U.S. rental norms and less able to view properties in person before signing. Harvard's police department maintains dedicated guidance directing students to the Off-Campus Housing site and external resources like the FTC.

Affordability & Boston vs. Cambridge dynamics

Budget-conscious students commonly aim to spend no more than 30–40% of monthly income on rent, which pushes many toward Allston, Brighton, and parts of Somerville rather than Cambridge proper.

Temporary / alternative housing pressure

In 2023, Harvard's housing office unexpectedly denied a large number of winter break housing applications, leaving international students on financial aid scrambling for temporary housing — a concrete example of this issue category.
